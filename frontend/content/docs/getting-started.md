Clai works in your browser. There's nothing to install, no account to create, and your document isn't uploaded anywhere.

## 1. Open Clai

Go to [Open Clai](/talk). You'll see one thing: a place to add a document.

## 2. Add an agreement

Drag a PDF onto the page, or click to choose one. If you don't have one handy, choose **Try a fictional sample agreement**. It's an invented services agreement written for testing.

Clai reads the PDF page by page, finds its sections and numbered clauses, and gets it ready for questions. A typical agreement takes a second or two. The header then shows the file name, the page count and how many passages Clai found.

## 3. Ask your first question

Type a question in plain language and press Enter. For the sample, try:

- *What is the notice period to end the agreement?*
- *Who owns the work produced?*
- *Is there a cap on liability?*

Clai answers by **quoting the clause** that settles the question, with its clause number and page.

## 4. Check the source

Click the clause reference under the answer (for example `↳ Clause 8.1 · p. 2`). The document panel opens on that page with the passage highlighted, so you can read it in context.

## 5. Keep going, or finish

Ask follow-ups. Clai links short questions such as *"and if they breach it?"* to your previous one. When you're done, choose **End session** to see a summary: what you asked, what the agreement says, and anything it didn't cover.

> Clai isn't legal advice. It shows you what the agreement says and where. For decisions that matter, read the clause in full and talk to a qualified lawyer.
