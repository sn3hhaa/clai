## Is Clai legal advice?

No. Clai shows you what an agreement says and where it says it. It doesn't interpret the law or judge whether terms are fair or enforceable. For decisions that matter, talk to a qualified lawyer.

## Is my document uploaded?

No. The public version reads the PDF in your browser. See [Privacy and security](/docs/privacy-and-security).

## Does it cost anything?

No. Clai is free and open source.

## Why did Clai say it couldn't find the answer?

Either the agreement doesn't use your key words at all (Clai names them), or no passage covered enough of your question. Try different wording, or check the closest passages it lists. Sometimes the agreement really doesn't say.

## Why are answers quotes rather than explanations?

So you can check them. A quote with its clause and page can be verified in seconds; a paraphrase can't. A server version can optionally write short answers, labelled as such.

## Can it read scanned documents?

Not yet. Scanned PDFs need text recognition (OCR), which is on the roadmap.

## Which browsers work?

Everything except voice input works in current Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox. Voice input works in Chrome, Edge and Safari. See [Voice experience](/docs/voice-experience).

## Does Clai remember my documents?

No. Closing the document or the tab discards it.

## Can I compare two agreements?

Not yet. It's planned.

## Can I run the full version myself?

Yes. The repository includes a server with meaning-based search, a reasoning graph and real-time voice. See the [technical reference](/docs/technical-reference).

## Limitations

- English, text-based PDFs only; no OCR or Word files yet.
- Clause numbers come from the document's own numbering; unusual layouts get page and heading citations instead.
- The browser version matches wording, not meaning. Very indirect questions can miss even when the answer is there.
- Voice depends on your browser's speech features.
